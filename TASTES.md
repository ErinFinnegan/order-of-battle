# Dad's taste in WWII books

What we've learned so far about the books he likes, from his 108 answers in the app (as
of October 16, 2026) and the things he's said. Update this as he sends more answers.

## In one paragraph

He wants the war told like an action movie: a storyteller following specific men
through one big, decisive operation. D-Day, Market Garden, Iwo Jima, the destroyers at
Samar, the carrier war and the bomber crews are his ground. Sweeping overviews feel
"like watching the news" to him, and first-person enlisted memoirs mostly miss. He has
read a great deal, so many "passes" just mean he's already covered that battle. Two
tones turn him off: **too sentimental** (hometown "band of brothers" stories) and **too
brutal** (long POW-camp survival ordeals).

## In his own words

- Nonfiction only. No novels, no sci-fi.
- Narrative histories are "like watching the news." He wants them to feel like action.
- Hugh Ambrose is great. *The Pacific* is Hugh's only WWII book, and he's heard it.
- He has read a lot about the 8th Air Force but doesn't always want another one.
- He has read a lot on the Battle of Britain, but a Goodreads list of titles was new to
  him and he found it interesting.
- He likes to pick a specific battle for the evening. Leyte Gulf was one.
- Some books are **"too sentimental."** *Band of Brothers* was one. Another was a
  "brothers" book that follows a group from high school into the war and home again;
  *The Bedford Boys* and *Brothers in Battle, Best of Friends* both fit that description
  and both got a thumbs down.
- Some are **"too brutal."** Japanese POW-camp survival stories were a slog to get
  through.

## What he likes

**Writers he keeps liking:** Ian W. Toll, James Bradley, Cornelius Ryan, Alex Kershaw,
John Wukovits, Adam Makos, Hugh Ambrose, Stephen Ambrose (*D-Day*, *The Wild Blue*).

**Books he's given a thumbs up (20):** *Flags of Our Fathers*, *Flyboys*, *The Longest
Day*, *A Bridge Too Far*, *D-Day: June 6, 1944*, *The Wild Blue*, *The Pacific*,
*Pacific Crucible*, *The Conquering Tide*, *The Last Stand of the Tin Can Sailors*,
*Masters of the Air*, *A Higher Call*, *Double Strike*, *The Liberator*, *The First
Wave*, *Against All Odds*, *To Hell and Back*, *Iwo Jima* (Newcomb), *Iwo Jima: Legacy
of Valor*, *One Square Mile of Hell*.

**Subjects that pull him in:** Iwo Jima and Tarawa; Sicily and Anzio; D-Day; destroyers
and the Battle off Samar; Medal of Honor stories (Audie Murphy above all); bomber crews
over Europe; Toll's carrier war.

## What he doesn't

- **First-person enlisted memoirs.** Disliked *Helmet for My Pillow*, *Parachute
  Infantry* and *Brothers in Battle, Best of Friends*; passed on *With the Old Breed*,
  *Company Commander* and *Guadalcanal Diary*. *To Hell and Back* is the big exception.
- **Easy Company and airborne books.** The strongest negative in his answers. Passed
  on *Band of Brothers* and *Beyond Band of Brothers*; disliked the spin-offs.
- **Generals' memoirs and biographies.** Passed on Eisenhower, Bradley, Patton (D'Este)
  and MacArthur (*American Caesar*).
- **Dense, big-picture histories.** Atkinson's trilogy and three Hornfischer books were
  only "OK." Passed on *Shattered Sword*, Frank's *Guadalcanal* and *The Rising Sun*.
- **The Battle of the Bulge and Guadalcanal.** Most books on either got a pass or worse.
- **Too sentimental.** Stories built around friendship and hometown loss: *Band of
  Brothers* and its spin-offs, *The Bedford Boys*, *Brothers in Battle, Best of
  Friends*. He wants the action, not the eulogy.
- **Too brutal.** Long prison-camp and survival ordeals, especially Japanese POW camps:
  *Unbroken*, *As Good As Dead*. *Ghost Soldiers* (the raid that freed POWs) was only
  "OK." A rescue or an escape told as an operation is probably fine; months of
  suffering in a camp is not.
- **Japanese-side or analytical takes** and oral-history collections.

## Battles he's probably done

These got repeated "Not this book" answers, which most likely means he's heard plenty:
Midway (*Incredible Victory*, *Shattered Sword*, Symonds), Pearl Harbor (*Brothers
Down*, *Ghosts of Honolulu*), the USS *Indianapolis* (*In Harm's Way*, *Abandon Ship!*),
Okinawa (*Crucible of Hell*, *The Ultimate Battle*, *Killing Ground on Okinawa*), and
the 8th Air Force memoirs (*Serenade to the Big Bird*, *The Mighty Eighth*, *The Fall of
Fortresses*).

## How he listens

- **Audible preferred, not required.** He still has it, and it's the most reliable way
  for him to get a book, so favor books with a confirmed Audible edition. A book that
  isn't on Audible can still be found elsewhere: Chirp (cheap sales, no subscription),
  Everand, Spotify, Audiobooks.com (a different catalog from Audible), LibriVox and the
  Internet Archive (older and out-of-print recordings).
- **Radio and documentaries count too.** BBC Sounds and CBC Listen have WWII
  documentaries and oral histories for a shorter evening.
- **Library apps (Libby, Hoopla) have been frustrating** because they depend on having
  a library card set up. Offer them last.
- **BARD** (free Library of Congress Talking Books) is the best bet for older books
  with no commercial audiobook, since his eyesight qualifies him.
- **Weak eyes, Android phone, and no love for the touchpad.** Big type, big buttons,
  as few choices on screen as possible. He doesn't read tags or match scores.

## Still learning

- Does he want more Battle of Britain, or was the Goodreads list just a curiosity?
  Every Battle of Britain book so far got a pass.
- The Pacific Army campaigns (New Guinea, the Philippines, Leyte on land) are mostly
  untested. McManus's trilogy is on his shelf to find out.
- Submarine memoirs (O'Kane, Fluckey) are untested. *Escape from the Deep* was a
  "read, don't remember."
- Where's the line on "too brutal"? *The Forgotten 500* (a rescue of downed airmen) is
  on his shelf as a test. *Lightning Down* (a P-38 pilot sent to Buchenwald) was taken
  off his phone shelf for now.
- Many of his 52 "Not this book" answers are ambiguous (not this battle? too
  sentimental? already read it?). The phone app's **Why not?** review asks him about
  each one; update this file once those answers come back.
